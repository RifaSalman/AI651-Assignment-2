# Question 1 — River Forecasting Proposal

The service issues a forecast every ten minutes. At issuance time τ it returns the water level one hour later at S1, S2, S3 and S4. The river graph is taken from the project figure. The reservoir releases into the main stem at S1. The monitored tributary, gauged at T1, joins between S1 and S2. The unmonitored tributary joins between S2 and S3. Rain gauges R1 and R2 sit on the right-hand side of the valley, toward that unmonitored catchment and the lower river. S4 is the last station before the downstream town. The town is not a forecast target.

The forecaster is a small directed graph model. Connectivity and distances are fixed inputs. Travel time is not. A lag kernel on each edge learns how long water takes to move, and it is allowed to change that lag when the river is high. A latent node stands in for the unmonitored tributary. The delivered product is the 0.5 quantile at τ+60 min, together with a 0.1–0.9 interval, at each of S1–S4.

## 1. Architecture diagram

Figure Q1 shows the data sources, the alignment step, the graph, the learned blocks, and the four forecasts. Tensor shapes include the batch axis B.

The nine nodes are Res, S1, T1, S2, U, S3, S4, R1 and R2. U is the unmonitored tributary and has no level sensor. Edges point downstream only:

- Res → S1 → S2 → S3 → S4
- T1 → S2
- U → S3
- R1 → U
- R2 → S3 and R2 → S4

History is aligned onto one 10-minute axis, τ−35 through τ (36 steps, 6 hours). The horizon is τ+1 through τ+6. Node features are stacked to (B, 36, 9, F). A shared GRU reads each node’s own history. Each edge then sends a mixture of lagged upstream states. Four message-passing steps are enough for a release at Res to reach S4, and for rain at R1 to reach S4 through U, inside one update. A six-step rollout follows. The only future input is the reservoir schedule that had already been announced at τ. Readout heads on S1–S4 produce (B, 6, 4, 3): six lead times, four stations, quantiles 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. The service publishes the slice at lead time 6.

## 2. Assumptions

Supplied facts, not assumptions: levels at S1–S4 and T1 every ten minutes; rainfall at R1 and R2 with timestamps and locations; recorded releases and future schedules announced before issuance, with announcement and effective times; station locations, connectivity and distances; no precise travel times; no measurement of the unmonitored tributary; no future rainfall and no future water levels at issuance. The figure fixes where the tributaries join.

A1. A 6-hour history is long enough for a disturbance at the reservoir or at R1 either to have reached S4 or to still be inside the window. Assignment 1’s teaser had a disturbance taking 12 samples, two hours, to travel from station 3 to station 4, so six hours covers the longer path from the dam to S4 with margin. If this is short, a release older than six hours is invisible and the forecast falls back on recent local level. The encoder length is 36 because of A1.

A2. Level records already share a 10-minute clock. Rainfall timestamps are irregular and are summed into those same bins, in millimetres per bin. Gauges are usually stored that way, and one clock is what lets a rain bin line up with a level bin.

A3. At least one year of record is available, covering a range of seasons and releases. A shorter record leaves the quantiles miscalibrated in unseen seasons. The design does not add a separate seasonal model; it adds time-of-day and day-of-year features (A3).

A4. Levels are stage in one vertical datum, in metres, with noise of a few centimetres, small next to a flood rise. There is no bias-correction block.

A5. Travel time shortens as the river rises, and the recent upstream level is a usable stand-in for that stage. The lag weights take distance and upstream level as inputs. A fixed lag is not used.

A6. R1 represents the headwaters of the unmonitored tributary, and R2 represents the lower right-hand valley that drains toward S3 and S4. The figure shows the gauges but not catchment polygons, so this assignment of edges is an assumption. It decides which forecasts rain is allowed to touch: R1 cannot move S1.

A7. Rain over the next hour is unknown and is not invented. That uncertainty is carried by the quantile width. No weather-model input is required for the system to run.

A8. A schedule whose announcement time is at or before τ is known exactly for the future steps. The actual release can differ. Past steps carry both the actual and the schedule; future steps carry the schedule only.

A9. One shared model serves all four stations. Upstream level is an input to the downstream forecast, and the same lag mechanism applies on every reach. Four separate models would drop that sharing.

A10. The authority needs a single level and a range. The 0.5 quantile is the issued level. The 0.1 and 0.9 quantiles are the issued range.

A11. A report usually arrives within ten minutes, but not always. A value is usable at τ only if its timestamp is at or before τ and its arrival time is at or before τ. Anything else is missing, not late-filled after the forecast has gone out.

A12. Channel distance is fixed. Travel time changes; the map does not. Distance is a static edge feature. The lag is learned.

A13. Backwater is negligible at the spacing in the figure, so edges point downstream only. A downstream constriction that holds water up at S3 would not be seen. That case is listed under limitations.

## 3. Forecasting task and data specification

Issuance time τ is the end of a 10-minute bin, and a forecast is issued at every such bin. The history window is the 36 bins τ−35, …, τ. The horizon is one hour: lead times of 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 minutes, written τ+1, …, τ+6. The required output is the level at S1–S4 at τ+6. The intermediate lead times are trained as well, so the one-hour forecast is the last step of a trajectory rather than a jump.

Information available at τ: levels and rain whose observation time and arrival time are both ≤ τ; actual releases already recorded by τ; the release schedule for future effective times, but only the version announced at or before τ; the static graph. Not available: future rain, future levels, the unmonitored inflow, and any schedule that has not been announced yet.

Alignment. Every series is placed on the same 10-minute index. A level stamped at time t is written into the bin that ends at t. Rain is the sum over (t−10 min, t]. Nodes do not share a spatial grid. They are stacked along a node axis in the fixed order Res, S1, T1, S2, U, S3, S4, R1, R2. Missing entries are 0, with the mask set to 0, so a missing level is not read as a water level of zero. The future schedule is a separate tensor. It is not concatenated into the history.

| Series | Shape | Axes | Time coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Level history, S1–S4 and T1 | (B, 36, 5) | batch, history step, station | τ−35 … τ |
| Level mask and staleness | (B, 36, 5) each | same | same; staleness in minutes since the last arrived observation |
| Rain history, R1 and R2 | (B, 36, 2) | batch, history step, gauge | millimetres in each bin τ−35 … τ |
| Rain mask | (B, 36, 2) | same | same |
| Actual release | (B, 36, 1) | batch, history step, reservoir | τ−35 … τ |
| Schedule that was in force in the past | (B, 36, 1) | same | the schedule known at that past time |
| Announced future schedule | (B, 6, 1) | batch, lead time, reservoir | τ+1 … τ+6, announcement time ≤ τ |
| Edge index | (2, E), E = 9 | source node, target node | static |
| Edge distance | (E,) | one distance per edge | static, along-river |
| Clock features | (B, 42, 4) | batch, history+horizon, sin/cos of hour and day-of-year | τ−35 … τ+6 |
| Output quantiles | (B, 6, 4, 3) | batch, lead time, station S1–S4, quantile 0.1 / 0.5 / 0.9 | τ+1 … τ+6 |
| Issued forecast | (B, 4) | batch, station | τ+6, the 0.5 quantile |

## 4. Model computations and design justification

Let V be the nine nodes and let z_{v,t} be the hidden state of node v at time t. Write x_{v,t} for the observed value (level, rain, or release), m_{v,t} for its mask, and s_{v,t} for staleness. Clock features are c_t. d_{uv} is the along-river distance of edge u→v.

Node encoder, shared across nodes of the same type (level station, rain gauge, reservoir, latent). For a node that has a sensor,

z_{v,t} = GRU( z_{v,t−1}, W [x_{v,t} ⊙ m_{v,t}, m_{v,t}, s_{v,t}, c_t] ).

The mask multiplies the value, so a missing entry contributes nothing through the value channel. U has no level; its input is the zero vector plus the clock, and it receives rain only through messages.

Edge message. For each downstream edge and each lag ℓ = 0, …, 35,

α_{uv,ℓ} = softmax_ℓ MLP(d_{uv}, z_{u,t}).

m_{u→v,t} = Σ_ℓ α_{uv,ℓ} z_{u, t−ℓ}.

The weights depend on distance and on the current upstream state, which is A5. They sum to one. A long edge can put its mass on a long lag. A high upstream state can move that mass toward shorter lags.

Message passing. Four rounds, with the GRU above run again so each round can mix a new set of incoming messages:

z_{v,t} ← GRU( z_{v,t}, Σ_{u: u→v} m_{u→v,t} ).

Four rounds match the longest path, Res to S4, which is four edges. Fewer rounds would make S4 wait several time steps before a release even enters its state, and that wait would be the same for a short reach and a long one. The lag kernel, not the depth, is what represents travel time.

Rollout. For lead k = 1, …, 6 there is no new level and no new rain. The reservoir node receives the announced release r_{τ+k} instead of a level. Other nodes receive only messages:

z_{v,τ+k} = GRU( z_{v,τ+k−1}, messages at τ+k, and r_{τ+k} if v is Res ).

Readout, stations s ∈ {S1, S2, S3, S4} and quantiles q ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}:

ŷ_{s,τ+k}^{(q)} = w_q · z_{s,τ+k} + b_q.

Training objective. On historical issuances the future levels are known after the fact, so they can be targets. They are never inputs. The pinball loss for quantile q is ρ_q(e) = q e if e ≥ 0, and (q−1) e otherwise, with e = y − ŷ. The objective is the mean of ρ_q over the batch, the four stations, the six leads and the three quantiles. The 0.5 term trains the issued level. The 0.1 and 0.9 terms train the range. No squared-error term is added: the range is the part the flood desk uses, and pinball trains it directly.

What is physical, and what is learned. The edge list, the downstream direction and the distances are taken from the river. They are not learned. The map from those inputs to a level is learned: the GRU weights, the lag MLP, and the readout. Mass conservation is not enforced. The unmonitored tributary and the unknown rating curves make a hard balance equation under-determined, so a violated balance would be fitted by distorting U. The directed graph is the physical constraint that remains: S1 does not depend on S4, T1 does not depend on S3, and rain does not flow upstream.

How each driver reaches each forecast.

- Reservoir. The future schedule enters only at Res. From there it crosses S1, S2, S3 and S4. A release announced at τ can change S1 inside the hour. It changes S4 inside the hour only if the learned lag is shorter than an hour. If the lag is longer, S4’s one-hour forecast moves only when the release is already in the history.
- Upstream main-stem level. S1’s state is an input to S2, S3 and S4. S4’s state is not an input to S1 (A13).
- Monitored tributary. T1 sends a message into S2, and from S2 onward to S3 and S4. It does not enter S1.
- Rain. R1 messages U, and U messages S3, so R1 affects S3 and S4 only. R2 messages S3 and S4 directly. Neither gauge touches S1 or S2. That is A6.
- Unmonitored tributary. U has no level. Its state is whatever the rain messages and the downstream residual leave it. S3 and S4 can rise when S2 does not, and that rise is attributed to U during training.

The one-hour product uses lead k = 6. Leads 1–5 are kept in the loss so the lag has a target at every step, not only at 60 minutes.

## 5. Operation and exceptional conditions

(i) Missing observations. The value is set to 0 and the mask to 0. Staleness counts the minutes since the last observation that did arrive. The GRU sees the mask and the staleness, so it can hold the last reliable state instead of treating a gap as a level of zero. Gaps are not filled with an interpolated level before the model runs. An interpolated value would look like a measurement.

(ii) Delayed observations. At issuance, any record whose arrival time is after τ is dropped, even if its sensor timestamp is earlier. It is a missing value for this forecast. When it arrives, a later issuance can use it inside the history window. A forecast already issued is not revised in this design. Revising it would break the ten-minute product the authority asked for.

(iii) Announced future releases. If the announcement time is ≤ τ, the scheduled release on τ+1, …, τ+6 is the future reservoir input. Schedules announced later are ignored until the next issuance. The effective time selects the lead step. A schedule that takes effect after τ+6 is outside this horizon and is not an input. During training, each past issuance is rebuilt with only the schedule that had been announced by that issuance, so the model does not train on releases it could not have known.

(iv) Scheduled versus actual releases. On past steps the actual release and the schedule then in force are both inputs, and so is their difference. On future steps only the schedule exists. A gate that opens late or only part way is something the model has seen as a residual in the history. The residual on the future step is not observed, so it widens the quantile range rather than shifting the input. If actuals are usually below the schedule, that pattern is in the training pairs and the 0.5 quantile can learn it. A one-off override that has no history still lands in the range, not in the point forecast.

Feasibility every ten minutes. The graph has 9 nodes and 9 edges. One forecast is a 36-step GRU, four message-passing rounds and a 6-step rollout, with a hidden width of a few tens. That is a fraction of a second on one CPU core. The ten-minute budget is set by when the gauges report, not by the forward pass. Training is offline, on stored issuances. The online step is the forward pass only. No benchmark is claimed beyond this size argument.

## 6. Uncertainty, limitations, and additional information

Unmonitored tributary. A storm on that branch raises S3 and S4 with no level gauge on the branch. The response is the latent node U, driven by R1 and R2, and a wider interval when rain is falling and the rise from S2 and T1 does not account for the rise at S3. What remains unresolved is rain that misses both gauges. The forecast is then late: it moves only after S3 itself has risen. Request: a level or flow gauge on the unmonitored tributary, at the confluence. It would turn U into an observed node and shorten the warning. The system still runs without it. Floods from that branch will be the weak case.

Changing travel times. A fixed delay would be early at high stage and late at low stage. The response is A5: the lag mixture depends on upstream level as well as distance. What remains unresolved is a change that level does not represent, such as ice, a debris dam, or a new cut. Request: a small set of travel-time measurements at low and high stage, or a velocity sensor at S2 and S3. They would check the learned lags and give a starting point for an unseen stage. The system still runs without them. Lags are learned from the historical record. An extreme stage outside that record will be off.

No future rainfall. Rain that starts after τ can reach a nearby station inside the hour. The response is to leave it out of the inputs and let the 0.1–0.9 interval carry it. The point forecast will not rise until a gauge has seen the rain. Request: a one-hour rainfall forecast, from radar or a numerical weather model, as an extra future input on R1 and R2, with its own uncertainty. The system still runs without it. Intervals stay wider, and a storm that begins after issuance is missed until it hits a gauge.

Schedule against actual. An unannounced gate change is invisible until the actual release is recorded. The response is the past residual feature and the quantile range. Request: gate telemetry within a minute of the movement, written into the actual-release series. The system still runs without it when operators stay close to the announced schedule.

Sensor datum and rating. A drifting zero looks like a slow change in level. Request: calibration dates and, if operations are ever done in discharge, the rating curves. The system still runs without them and forecasts the level as reported. It does not convert level to flow.

Backwater and the town. A13 drops upstream edges. If a bridge or a constriction downstream of S4 holds water, S4 can rise for a reason that is not in the graph. Request: a note of control structures and their operating rules. The system still runs without them. This error is local to the affected station.

The forecast remains usable for the four stations with the data already offered. The extra requests improve the unmonitored branch, the travel-time check, and the next-hour rain. None of them is required for a forecast to be issued every ten minutes.
