# Question 1 — River Forecasting Proposal

The forecaster uses a shared LSTM on each series for time, then self-attention across series embeddings for space (temporal-then-spatial composition). Sensors are irregular points with known river connectivity, so 2D convolution / ConvLSTM on a grid is not used. Graph message-passing layers are not used. Only information that is available at issuance τ enters the model. The product is the water level at S1–S4 at τ+60 min.

## 1. Architecture diagram


Figure Q1 shows each data source, timestamp alignment, the LSTM, the attention mixer, and the S1–S4 forecasts, with main tensor shapes labelled.

Sources: levels S1–S4 and T1; rainfall R1 and R2 with native timestamps and locations; reservoir actual releases and announced schedules (announcement and effective times); static connectivity and along-river distances.

Alignment: all series map onto one 10-minute clock of bin end-times. History is τ−35 … τ. Horizon is τ+1 … τ+6. A value enters bin t only if its observation/effective time falls in that bin and it is available by τ. Each series is projected to a common feature width before the LSTM.

Blocks: shared LSTM over 36 steps per series; two self-attention blocks over the series tokens, with additive biases from distances and upstream connectivity; station-and-lead readout. Output Ŷ is (B, 4, 6); the published slice is (B, 4) at lead +60 min.

## 2. Assumptions

Supplied facts: levels at S1–S4 and T1 every ten minutes; rainfall at R1 and R2 with timestamps and locations; recorded releases and future schedules announced before issuance, with announcement and effective times; station locations, connectivity and distances; no precise travel times; no unmonitored-tributary measurement; no future rainfall or future levels at issuance. The figure fixes where the tributaries join.

A1. History length. Six hours (36 bins) covers dam→S4 travel with margin relative to Assignment 1’s two-hour station-3→4 example. Shorter history would hide older releases. Sets LSTM length to 36.

A2. Shared 10-minute clock. Levels are already on that grid. Rain timestamps are summed into (t−10 min, t]; a bin with no reading is 0 with rain-mask 0 (not a carried rate). Release effective times use the same bins.

A3. Observation vs arrival. Levels, rain and actual releases need observation/effective time ≤ τ and arrival time ≤ τ. Late packets are dropped, not waited on.

A4. Record length. At least one year of archived issuances. Sin/cos of hour and day-of-year are inputs. No separate seasonal model.

A5. Measurement quality and scaling. Stage is in one datum, metres, with centimetre-scale noise; there is no bias-correction block. Each series is standardized with its training mean and standard deviation and inverted on the issued forecast, so stage, rain and release can share one network width.

A6. Travel time. Not a fixed delay and not distance divided by a constant speed. Delayed effects must appear in the LSTM history. Along-river distances bias attention among the reservoir, S1–S4 and T1; gauge-to-station distance for R1/R2 is straight-line from the given coordinates. Neither distance is turned into a travel time.

A7. Rain catchments. R1 informs the unmonitored headwaters; R2 the lower valley toward S3/S4. Bias reduces R1→S1 weight.

A8. No future rain; point forecast. Future rain is not invented. The training loss is mean squared error on level (missing targets dropped). No quantile head and no separate physical simulator.

A9. Announced schedules. A release is treated as piecewise constant between effective times. A future bin is usable only if announcement time ≤ τ and effective time falls in τ+1 … τ+6. When training on the archive, each past issuance is rebuilt with only announcements that were public at that issuance.

A10. Shared model. One LSTM stack and one attention stack for all four stations.

A11. Published product. Train leads +10 … +60 min; publish only +60 min.

A12. Residual S3−S2. Kept when both masks are 1, as a cue for the unmonitored branch, not a measured inflow.

A13. Backwater. Treated as negligible; biases favour upstream→downstream influence.

## 3. Forecasting task and data specification

Issuance time τ is the end of a 10-minute bin. History: τ−35 … τ. Horizon: τ+1 … τ+6 (one hour). Outputs: levels at S1–S4 for each lead; service product is the four levels at τ+6.

Available at τ: levels/rain with obs and arrival ≤ τ; actual releases with effective and arrival ≤ τ; schedule rows with announcement ≤ τ and effective time in (τ, τ+60 min]; static layout. Not available: future rain, future levels, unmonitored inflow, schedules announced after τ, unarrived packets.

Alignment: (1) shared bin end-times; (2) levels into their native bin; (3) rain summed by timestamp into bins (empty ⇒ mask 0); (4) actual release by effective time, kept if arrival ≤ τ; (5) schedule to past bins or future leads by effective time, gated by announcement; (6) no spatial raster—series stay separate tokens; (7) batch B indexes issuances.

## 4. Model computations and design justification

Temporal: for series i and bin t, u_{i,t} = [x_{i,t} ⊙ m_{i,t}, m_{i,t}, s_{i,t}, c_t],

h_{i,t} = LSTM(h_{i,t−1}, W_type u_{i,t}), e_i = h_{i,τ},

shared by series type. Future schedule r with known-mask κ → e_rel = MLP([r ⊙ κ; κ]). Token list

Z⁰ = [e_{S1}, e_{S2}, e_{S3}, e_{S4}, e_{T1}, e_{R1}, e_{R2}, e_{res}, e_rel].

Spatial: two transformer encoder blocks (4 heads, width 64, FFN 4d). Missing series get score −∞. Logit bias b_{ij} = λ_dist · φ(distance_{ij}) + λ_up · 1[i upstream of j].

Readout: q_{s,k} = W_q [Z²_s; lead_k; staleness_s], ŷ_{s,τ+k} = wᵀ Attn(q_{s,k}, Z²) + b.

Objective: MSE over stations and leads; drop missing future targets. Future levels are never inputs.

Fixed: series list, timestamp rules, connectivity, distances, unannounced ⇒ invisible. Learned: LSTM, attention, biases, readout. No mass-conservation constraint.

Drivers. S1: own history plus reservoir actual/schedule (e_res, e_rel); rain only weakly via A7. S2: S1 and T1 as upstream tokens, then the reservoir with a longer lag. S3: S2 stem, rain, residual S3−S2 for the unmonitored branch, and the reservoir. S4: same upstream set through attention in one hop, with S3 as the nearest level cue. Inflow that has not reached S3 by τ remains invisible.

## 5. Operation and exceptional conditions

(i) Missing: value 0, mask 0, staleness updated; no interpolation; fully missing series masked in attention.

(ii) Delayed: arrival > τ ⇒ drop for this issuance; later issuances may use it; no revision of issued forecasts.

(iii) Announced future releases: include in lead k only if announcement ≤ τ and effective time in that lead bin; training rebuilds each past issuance with only schedules known at that τ.

(iv) Schedule vs actual: both on past bins when available; future leads use announced schedule only.

Feasibility: length-36 LSTM on eight series, two attention blocks on nine tokens, 24 queries—well under ten minutes on CPU. Budget is packet arrival, not compute.

## 6. Uncertainty, limitations, and additional information

Unmonitored tributary. Impact: under-forecast until S3 rises or rain correlates. Response: residual + R1. Request: confluence gauge. Usable without it: yes.

Changing travel times. Impact: mis-timed waves at unseen stage. Response: data-driven LSTM/attention, no hard delay. Request: travel-time checks at low/high stage. Usable without them: yes.

No future rainfall. Impact: post-τ storms missed. Request: one-hour rain forecast token. Usable without it: yes.

Schedule vs actual. Impact: unannounced gate change invisible until actual arrives. Request: faster gate telemetry. Usable without it: yes if operators follow the schedule.

Datum/rating. Request: calibration dates / rating curves. Usable without them: yes (forecast reported stage).

Backwater. Request: control-structure notes. Usable without them: yes; error local.

Clock skew on arrival stamps. Request: authenticated centre clock. Usable with conservative A3: yes.
