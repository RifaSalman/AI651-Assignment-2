Q2.5 (written before any mirror, rotation, or speed experiment)

Model A will fail the mirror test and the rotation test. It is trained only on ordinary 1× clips, and a plain ConvLSTM has no mirror or 90° symmetry built in. Figure 1(a) is not flat: every 20° bin is occupied, so a reflected or rotated direction is not a completely new kind of motion, but the fullest bin is about 3.1 times the emptiest. Mirroring the directions moves about 13% of the probability mass, and a 90° rotation moves about 18%. I expect the network to tie its forecast to the digit shapes and to the directions it saw most often. Rotation should be worse than mirroring, because a 90° turn changes what a digit looks like more, and it sends every direction into a different part of the histogram.

On speed, model A should mostly keep up at 2× and break at 3×. One step reaches 8 original pixels: the input passes through two 3×3 convolutions on the 16×16 grid, each grid step is 4 original pixels, and the 4×4 patch width is not added. Figure 1(b) has median speed 3.32 pixels per frame and a 90th percentile of 4.12. Twice the median is 6.64, still inside 8 pixels. Three times the median is 9.95, which is beyond an axis-aligned step of 8. The fast tail at 2× is about 8.2 pixels per frame, so a few 2× clips may already smear, but the clear breakdown should be at 3×.
